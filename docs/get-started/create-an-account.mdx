You create a Cloider account by signing in with your Google or GitHub account on [cloider.com](https://cloider.com). There is no separate Cloider password and no sign up form: the first sign in creates your account. Then you claim a $25 welcome credit for a 14-day trial from the dashboard.

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Sign in with** | Google or GitHub (OAuth). Your email address from the provider becomes your Cloider identity |
| **Trial** | $25 of credit for 14 days, as advertised on [cloider.com](https://cloider.com). The 14 days start when you claim it in the dashboard |
| **Limits** | None per product: the credit is the only limit. Use it for any mix of clusters, databases, apps, buckets and registries |
| **After the trial** | Add funds to keep going. When the account has no funds left, compute pauses, and after 14 days resources are removed |
| **Session length** | A dashboard session lasts 24 hours, then you sign in again |

## Before you begin

- A **Google** account or a **GitHub** account. Cloider uses the email address of that account. For GitHub, that is your primary email address, and it must be verified on GitHub.
- A web browser.

:::note[One email address, one Cloider user]
Cloider identifies you by your email address, in lowercase. If your Google account and your GitHub account use the same email address, both sign you in to the same Cloider user and the same account. If they use different addresses, you get two separate Cloider users.
:::

## Sign up

1. Go to [cloider.com](https://cloider.com).
2. Click **Login / Register** in the top navigation bar.
3. In the **Login Or Register** dialog, click **Sign in with Google** or **Sign in with Github**.
4. On the Google or GitHub page, choose your account and approve the request. Cloider reads your name, email address and profile picture.
5. You return to cloider.com and land on the dashboard.

## Your user and your account

Signing up creates two things that share your email address:

- **Your user** is you, the person who signs in.
- **Your account** is where resources live and where usage is paid from. You are its **Owner**.

You can later be invited to other people's accounts as a member, and you can invite others into yours. Each resource belongs to exactly one account. Read [Accounts and roles](/concepts/accounts-and-roles) for how users, accounts, members and roles fit together, and [Switch accounts](/account/switch-accounts) for moving between them.

## The welcome credit

A new account gets a 14-day trial with **$25 of credit**. You claim it from the prompt at the top of the dashboard, and the 14 days start when you do, so you can sign up first and try Cloider later.

1. Open the dashboard on your own account. The prompt says *Start your 14-day trial. Get $25 of credit to try clusters, databases, apps, buckets and registries.*
2. Click **Start trial**. The dashboard confirms *Your trial has started* and the sidebar shows the $25 in your balance.

**Later** hides the prompt until you open the dashboard again. The prompt only appears on your own account, so being invited to someone else's account doesn't start your trial. Each account can claim the welcome credit once, and only in the dashboard.

Until the credit is claimed, creating a resource fails with:

```text
Claim your $25 welcome credit from the dashboard to start your trial.
```

### What the trial includes

During the trial you can use every product, in any mix: there is no limit on how many clusters, databases, apps, buckets or registry projects you create. Usage is paid from the $25 credit as it happens, at the normal prices, so the [Usage](/account/usage) page shows what each resource costs. The sidebar shows what is left of the credit, and the account wide limits are listed in [Limits and quotas](/reference/limits-and-quotas).

### What happens when the trial ends

The trial ends when the 14 days are over or the $25 is spent, whichever comes first. What is left of the credit after 14 days is no longer available. To keep your resources running, the account Owner [adds funds](/account/billing#add-funds) before then.

If the account has no funds left:

1. Cloider emails the Owner before it happens: 7 days and 1 day before the funds run out at the current usage, or when the credit's expiry would leave the account without funds.
2. The account is **suspended**. Its clusters, databases and apps are paused. Buckets and registry projects keep working.
3. After 14 days suspended without a top-up, the account's resources are removed, after two warning emails.

Adding funds makes the account active again and resumes what the suspension paused. See [What happens when your funds run out?](/account/billing#what-happens-when-your-funds-run-out) for the details, and [Billing model](/concepts/billing-model) for how usage is metered and paid.

## Troubleshooting

### I signed in with GitHub and see an empty account

Your GitHub account probably uses a different email address than the Google account you used before, so Cloider created a second user. Sign out (account menu, **Logout**) and sign in with the provider you used originally.

### I see "Select an account" instead of the dashboard

You are a member of at least one other account, so Cloider asks which account to open. Pick one. See [Switch accounts](/account/switch-accounts).

### "Login session lost!"

Your session expired (sessions last 24 hours) or you signed out in another tab. Click **Reload page** and sign in again. See [Troubleshooting](/reference/troubleshooting) for more sign in problems.
