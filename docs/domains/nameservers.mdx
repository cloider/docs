Cloider's nameservers are `ns1.cloider.com`, `ns2.cloider.com`, `ns3.cloider.com` and `ns4.cloider.com`. When you set
them as your domain's nameservers at your registrar, Cloider answers DNS for the domain with the records you manage in
Cloider. This page shows how to change the nameservers at popular registrars, how to check the change, and exactly how
Cloider's nameservers answer queries.

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Nameservers** | `ns1.cloider.com`, `ns2.cloider.com`, `ns3.cloider.com`, `ns4.cloider.com` |
| **Set them at** | Your registrar, where the domain is registered |
| **Time to take effect** | Usually minutes to a few hours, up to 48 hours |
| **What they answer with** | The records on the domain's **DNS records** list in Cloider, plus the domain's `SOA` and `NS` records |
| **Record types answered** | `A`, `AAAA`, `CNAME`, `MX`, `TXT`, `NS`, `SOA`, `SRV`, `PTR`, `CAA`, `HTTPS`, `SPF`. `ALIAS` records are answered as `A` and `AAAA` |
| **Transport** | UDP and TCP on port 53 |
| **DNSSEC** | Not supported. Turn DNSSEC off at your registrar before switching |
| **Cost** | No charge |

## Where to find the nameservers

The four names are the same for every domain and every account. You find them:

- **Dashboard**: on the **Activate Cloider DNS** step when you add a custom domain, and in the **Name servers** section
  of every custom domain's page, each with a copy button. The text reads **Login into your domain provider and change
  your name servers to the following:**.
- **CLI**: `cloider domain create example.com` prints them.
- **Terraform**: the `nameservers` attribute of the [`cloider_options`](/terraform/data-sources/options) data source.
- **API**: `nameservers` in `GET https://cloider.com/api/options`.

## Before you change the nameservers

1. **Add the domain to Cloider.** See [Add a custom domain](/domains/add-a-custom-domain).
2. **Copy the records the domain already uses** into Cloider, especially `MX` and `TXT` records for email. Once the
   nameservers switch, only Cloider's records answer. See
   [Recreate your existing DNS records](/domains/add-a-custom-domain#step-2-recreate-your-existing-dns-records).
3. **Turn off DNSSEC** if it is enabled. At most registrars this is a DNSSEC switch or a list of DS records to delete.
   Cloider's nameservers don't sign answers, and a domain that keeps a DS record while its nameservers don't sign
   stops resolving for every resolver that validates DNSSEC.
4. **Lower the TTL** of important records at your current DNS provider a day ahead, if you can. That shortens the time
   some visitors see old answers.

## Change the nameservers at your registrar

At every registrar the change is the same: find the domain's nameserver setting, switch from the registrar's own DNS to
custom nameservers, enter the four Cloider names, remove any others, and save. Registrars rename their menus from time
to time, so if a label below doesn't match, look for **Nameservers**, **DNS** or **Custom DNS** in the domain's
settings, or search your registrar's help center for "change nameservers".

<Tabs groupId="registrar" queryString="registrar">
<TabItem value="namecheap" label="Namecheap" default>

1. Sign in to Namecheap and open **Domain List**.
2. Click **Manage** next to the domain.
3. In the **Nameservers** section, change the dropdown from **Namecheap BasicDNS** to **Custom DNS**.
4. Enter `ns1.cloider.com` and `ns2.cloider.com`, click **Add Nameserver** and enter `ns3.cloider.com` and
   `ns4.cloider.com`.
5. Click the green check mark to save.

Namecheap says the change can take up to 48 hours, and it is often visible much sooner.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="godaddy" label="GoDaddy">

1. Sign in to GoDaddy and open your **Domain Portfolio**.
2. Select the domain, then open its **DNS** settings and the **Nameservers** tab.
3. Click **Change Nameservers** and choose to use your own nameservers.
4. Enter the four Cloider nameservers, adding fields as needed.
5. Save, and confirm the warning that GoDaddy's DNS records will no longer be used.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="cloudflare" label="Cloudflare">

**Domains registered with Cloudflare Registrar** use Cloudflare's own nameservers, and Cloudflare doesn't let you
replace them with another provider's. Keep DNS at Cloudflare and point the host names you serve at Cloider from there,
as described in [Keep your current DNS provider](/domains/add-a-custom-domain#keep-your-current-dns-provider). Set those
records to **DNS only** (grey cloud).

**Domains registered elsewhere but using Cloudflare DNS**: change the nameservers at the registrar where the domain is
registered, using that registrar's steps. Cloudflare's dashboard can't change them for you.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="squarespace" label="Squarespace Domains">

Squarespace Domains took over domains registered with Google Domains.

1. Sign in to Squarespace and open the **Domains** dashboard.
2. Select the domain and open its **DNS** settings.
3. Find **Domain nameservers** and choose to use custom nameservers.
4. Enter the four Cloider nameservers and save.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="route53" label="Amazon Route 53">

For domains registered with Route 53 (the registrar part of the service):

1. Open the Route 53 console and choose **Registered domains**.
2. Select the domain and choose the action to edit its name servers.
3. Replace the four `awsdns` name servers with `ns1.cloider.com`, `ns2.cloider.com`, `ns3.cloider.com` and
   `ns4.cloider.com`.
4. Save the change.

A Route 53 hosted zone for the domain stops being used once the name servers change. Delete it when you no longer need
it, to stop paying for it.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="other" label="Other registrars">

Porkbun, Gandi, IONOS, OVHcloud, Hostinger, Dynadot and most other registrars work the same way:

1. Open the domain's settings at the registrar.
2. Find **Nameservers** (sometimes under **DNS**) and choose custom or external nameservers.
3. Enter all four Cloider nameservers and remove the registrar's.
4. Save.

Some registrars also offer "glue records" or "child nameservers" in the same area. You don't need them for Cloider.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

:::note[Country code domains with strict registry checks]
Some country code registries test new nameservers before accepting a change, and expect them to answer for the domain
with `SOA` and `NS` records. Cloider's nameservers answer both within seconds of the domain being added. If your
registrar still reports a failed nameserver check, keep DNS at your current provider and point the host names at
Cloider from there, or contact [support](/account/support).
:::

## Does the domain need NS records?

No. Cloider's nameservers answer `NS` queries for the domain itself with `ns1.cloider.com` to `ns4.cloider.com`, and
`SOA` queries with the domain's `SOA` record, within seconds of the domain being added. Tools and services that ask "which
nameservers does `example.com` use?" get the right answer from Cloider without any record of yours.

An `NS` record you add at the apex replaces those four in the answer, so leave the name blank only for nameservers the
domain is really delegated to. `NS` records on another name, such as `dev`, are answered as you add them.

## Check that the nameservers have changed

There are two places to look: the registry, which says which nameservers the domain uses, and Cloider's nameservers,
which answer with your records.

```bash
# What the .com registry hands out (use your TLD's servers for other TLDs)
dig example.com NS @a.gtld-servers.net +noall +authority

# Follow the whole chain from the root, for any TLD
dig +trace example.com A

# What Cloider's nameservers answer for the domain
dig @ns1.cloider.com example.com A +short
```

```text title="Output"
example.com.		172800	IN	NS	ns1.cloider.com.
example.com.		172800	IN	NS	ns2.cloider.com.
example.com.		172800	IN	NS	ns3.cloider.com.
example.com.		172800	IN	NS	ns4.cloider.com.
```

On Windows, use `nslookup` against Cloider's nameserver, and your registrar's dashboard or a `whois` lookup for the
registry's view:

```bash
nslookup -type=A example.com ns1.cloider.com
```

If the registry still lists the old nameservers, the change hasn't been saved or published yet: check the registrar.
If it lists Cloider's nameservers but your browser still sees the old site, resolvers still have old answers cached.
Wait, or test with a public resolver such as `dig @1.1.1.1 example.com`.

## How Cloider's nameservers answer

Cloider's nameservers are **authoritative only**: they answer questions about domains added to Cloider, from the
records you add, and they don't look up other names for you. Knowing their behavior helps you read `dig` output.

| Behavior | What you see |
| --- | --- |
| **Authoritative answers** | Answers for domains added to Cloider carry the `aa` flag. Recursion isn't offered (no `ra` flag), so don't use `ns1.cloider.com` as your computer's resolver |
| **Other domains** | A query for a domain that isn't added to Cloider gets `REFUSED` |
| **Record types** | `A`, `AAAA`, `CNAME`, `MX`, `TXT`, `NS`, `SOA`, `SRV`, `PTR`, `CAA`, `HTTPS` and `SPF` queries are answered. Other types get an empty answer. An [`ALIAS`](/domains/dns-records#alias-record) record answers `A` and `AAAA` queries with its target's addresses, and `SERVFAIL` when the target can't be looked up |
| **TTL** | Each answer carries the TTL you set on the record, 60 to 172800 seconds. An `ALIAS` answer carries the target's TTL when that is lower, at least 30 seconds and at most an hour |
| **Name exists, type doesn't** | Status `NOERROR` with an empty answer (NODATA), for example an `AAAA` query for a name that has only an `A` record |
| **Name doesn't exist** | Status `NXDOMAIN`. The apex always exists, since it has `SOA` and `NS` records even before you add any |
| **CNAME** | A query for a name that has a CNAME returns the CNAME. When the target is in the same domain, its records (or an `ALIAS`'s addresses) follow in the same answer, up to 8 CNAMEs deep. Otherwise your resolver follows the CNAME itself, also to another domain on Cloider |
| **Wildcards** | A record named `*` answers for any name below the domain that has no record of the queried type, under the queried name. It doesn't answer for the apex |
| **TXT** | Each value is a TXT record of its own. A value longer than 255 characters is sent as several strings in one record, as the DNS standard requires |
| **SPF type** | Records of type `SPF` are also returned for `TXT` queries, because mail servers look SPF up as TXT |
| **SOA** | Every domain has an `SOA` record naming `ns1.cloider.com` as the primary nameserver and `hostmaster.cloider.com` as the contact. `NXDOMAIN` and empty answers carry it in the authority section, so resolvers cache them for at most 300 seconds |
| **NS** | The apex answers with `ns1.cloider.com` to `ns4.cloider.com`, unless you add `NS` records there |
| **Transport** | UDP and TCP on port 53. A UDP answer too big for the client comes back truncated, and the client asks again over TCP |
| **DNSSEC** | Answers aren't signed |

## Move your DNS away from Cloider

To move the domain to another DNS provider later:

1. Recreate every record at the new provider. List them with `cloider dns list example.com` or export them with
   `cloider dns list example.com -o json`.
2. Change the nameservers at your registrar to the new provider's.
3. Wait at least 48 hours, so resolvers stop asking Cloider.
4. Delete the domain in Cloider if you no longer route it with targets. See
   [Delete a domain](/domains/delete-a-domain).

The domain's page in the dashboard reminds you of this order: **Make sure to configure your domain with another DNS
provider before deleting this domain.**
